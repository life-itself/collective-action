# Names of collective action problems

## Notes on the table

- **Chicken.** Osborne and Rubinstein give both names:

> Hawk–Dove is known also as "Chicken".

— Martin J. Osborne and Ariel Rubinstein, *A Course in Game Theory* (1994), p. 30

- **Tragedy of the commons.** Ostrom (*Governing the Commons*, 1990, pp. 217–218, n. 2) notes that Garrett Hardin's model translates easily into the prisoner's dilemma structure. Open access is the prisoner's dilemma case; limited-access commons often aren't one (p. 48). See [[Taking and building]].
- **"Free-rider problem"** is often used for every collective action problem. Ostrom (pp. 2–7) shows that the tragedy of the commons, the prisoner's dilemma and Olson's logic of collective action all rest on free riding. Strictly, free riding in a [[Volunteer's dilemma]] or a [[Stag hunt]] has different consequences and different fixes.
- **"Social dilemma"** is an umbrella term for the whole family, not a synonym for the prisoner's dilemma: Kollock (1998) covers the prisoner's dilemma, assurance and chicken under it.
- **Privileged group** is only roughly harmony: Olson (p. 50, n. 70) notes that a privileged group could fail if each refuses "on the mistaken assumption that the others would provide it", though he doubts it would last. That is the volunteer's dilemma.
- **Arms races and the security dilemma** fit the prisoner's dilemma or the stag hunt depending on the payoffs. See [[Prisoner's dilemma vs stag hunt]].
- **Moloch** is from Scott Alexander, "Meditations on Moloch" (*Slate Star Codex*, 30 July 2014). It is good branding for the battle line. Applied to every collective action problem, it misses the patterns whose fix is assurance or allocation rather than changing everyone's payoffs ([[Patterns of collective action]]).
- **Numbering.** In Rapoport and Guyer's taxonomy of 78 ordinal 2×2 games, the prisoner's dilemma is game 12, assurance 61, chicken 66, the privileged game 6 and the altruist's dilemma 9 (as used by Heckathorn 1996, p. 258).
