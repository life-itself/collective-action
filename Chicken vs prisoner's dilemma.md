# Chicken vs prisoner's dilemma

Two neighbors and a job. Payoffs ordinal, 4 best to 1 worst, (row, column).

**[[Prisoner's Dilemma]]: draining a shared field.** Each ditch helps the whole field, but less than it costs the person who digs it. A lone digger's effort is mostly wasted: they end up worse off than if nobody had dug.

| | Neighbor digs | Neighbor doesn't |
|---|---|---|
| **I dig** | 3, 3 | **1**, 4 |
| **I don't** | 4, 1 | **2**, 2 |

**[[Chicken (game)]]: a snowdrift across a shared lane.** Either of them alone can clear it. A lone shoveller is better off than if both stay indoors and nobody gets out.

| | Neighbor shovels | Neighbor doesn't |
|---|---|---|
| **I shovel** | 3, 3 | **2**, 4 |
| **I don't** | 4, 2 | **1**, 1 |

Where the neighbor works (left column), the two games are the same: I'd rather not (4 > 3, [[Greed and fear|greed]]). The difference is in the right column, where the neighbor doesn't: acting alone is worse than nobody acting in the drainage case (1 < 2) and better in the snowdrift (2 > 1).

> a PD is transformed into a chicken game.

— Douglas Heckathorn, "The Dynamics and Dilemmas of Collective Action" (1996), p. 258, of what happens when the sucker's payoff rises above mutual defection

## What the swap changes

| | Drainage (prisoner's dilemma) | Snowdrift (chicken) |
|---|---|---|
| Equilibria | One: nobody digs. Not digging is best whatever the neighbor does. | Two: one shovels, the other doesn't. If I'm sure you won't, I will. Plus a mixed one where nobody does it some of the time. |
| Typical outcome | The field stays wet | The lane usually gets cleared, by someone |
| Failure | Universal shirking | The wrong person pays; waiting; standoffs; in big groups, nobody acts ([[Volunteer's dilemma]]) |
| Conflict | Me against the group | Me against you: who pays |
| Fix | Change payoffs for everyone: sanctions, rewards, repetition | Allocate: name someone, take turns, split the cost (when one person is enough) |
| Assurance | Doesn't help: if you dig, not digging still pays more | Backfires: if I know you'll shovel, I wait |

Allocation is the distinctive fix only when one actor is enough. When everyone acting is best, chicken is fixed much like a prisoner's dilemma (Dixit, Skeath and Reiley, *Games of Strategy*, 4th ed., 2015, p. 449). Taylor (*The Possibility of Cooperation*, 1987, p. 43) judges the prospects for cooperation "a little more promising" in chicken than in the prisoner's dilemma.

## Where they blur

- The symptom can look the same: nothing gets done. The pattern is in the payoffs, not the outcome.
- Small changes move a case across the line. Heckathorn's game space (p. 257) puts the two in neighboring regions; chicken appears when the first contribution produces most of the good (p. 258).
- Repetition: Taylor (p. 40) notes that the n-person prisoner's dilemma supergame can itself be a chicken game.
