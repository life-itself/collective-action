A single sketch plus a short write-up. The metaphor is a canoe (or tandem bike): someone stops paddling and still arrives, as long as the others keep going. If everyone stops, the group doesn't get there.

The write-up is basic and the framing is simple. Value here is as a ready-made graphic and an example of how the idea is presented to a general audience.

## Excerpt

> Whenever one person cannot be excluded from the benefits that others provide, each person is motivated not to contribute to the joint effort, but to free-ride on the efforts of others.

— Elinor Ostrom, as quoted on the Sketchplanations page. Original source not named there; check against Ostrom directly before reusing.

## Key points

- Definition: benefiting from a group effort without contributing to it.
- Examples listed: group school projects, shared student kitchens, Wikipedia, fishing quotas, climate action, vaccination rates, tax compliance.
- Closing note: free-riding is common, but most people keep contributing.
