# Cases on the pattern map

How real cases sort into the three patterns of [[Patterns of collective action]]. Names: **battle line** ([[Prisoner's Dilemma]]), **stag hunt** (assurance game; see [[Stag hunt]]), **chicken** ([[Volunteer's dilemma]]; the two-player form is [[Chicken (game)]]). The fourth cell, **no problem**, is where cooperating pays anyway.

The map is built from two questions ([[Greed and fear]]):

- **Greed:** if you pull your weight, do I gain by slacking?
- **Fear:** if I pull mine alone, do I lose?

![[assets/case-map.svg]]

Clean cases are written inside their cell. The numbered cases fit more than one pattern: a dot on a border fits both cells, the dot in the middle fits all three, and an outlined dot is the same case in a second cell. The dashed diagonal joins the stag hunt and chicken: cases on it fit both, mostly because of a many-player threshold. The order of dots along a border means nothing.

## Clean cases

| Pattern | Taking (restraint) | Building (contribution) |
|---|---|---|
| **Battle line** | Newfoundland cod; Moloch and races to the bottom | Open source, research, the public-goods experiment |
| **Stag hunt** | Northern Rock bank run | Protests and revolutions; the union drive in *Norma Rae*; the *Potemkin* mutiny |
| **Chicken** | Brinkmanship; *Dr. Strangelove*; who gets the best fishing sites at Alanya, Turkey (Ostrom) | A person collapses in the street |
| **No problem** | Probably leaded petrol (each country gains from its own phase-out) | Meerkat sentinels |

## Cases that fit more than one pattern

Thirteen cases fit two patterns or more. The last column says what moves them between patterns.

| Case | Patterns | Taking or building | What moves it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hoplite battle line | Battle line / stag hunt | Building | Each man gains by easing back (battle line), but the line holds only if enough stand (stag hunt). Krentz's casualty ratio suggests a threshold. |
| Herds scatter; buffalo turn on the lions | Battle line / stag hunt | Building | Group defence works only if enough stand. |
| Anglo-German naval race (before 1914) | Battle line / stag hunt | Taking | Whether each side wants a bigger navy anyway (battle line) or builds only because it fears the other's (stag hunt: the security dilemma). |
| Limited Test Ban Treaty | Battle line / stag hunt | Taking | As the naval race. Verification set what could be banned. |
| Heartbleed and OpenSSL (xkcd 2347) | Battle line / chicken | Building | Many small users, each with a small benefit: battle line. One big firm that gains enough to fund it alone: chicken. After 2014 big firms funded it, possibly a case of one big player solving it (not yet checked). |
| Wikipedia and the open source maintainer | No problem / battle line / chicken | Building | Many small edits are worth making for their own sake (no problem). Maintaining the core is not (battle line), unless one party picks it up (chicken). |
| The Great Stink (London, 1858) | Battle line, then no problem | Building | Those who could fix the sewers didn't bear the cost until the smell reached Parliament, which then gained enough to act alone. Partly a [[Principal-agent problem]]. |
| Playground bystanders | Stag hunt / chicken | Building | "We'd all have stood up if we knew the others would" is a stag hunt. "One person telling the head teacher is enough" is chicken. |
| Plataean breakout (428 BC) | Stag hunt / chicken | n/a | Enough must attempt it for it to work (stag hunt); too many makes it riskier. |
| Liberum veto | Stag hunt / chicken | n/a | Any one noble could block the Polish parliament, so each hold-out plays chicken. Needing every vote makes it a stag hunt where the least willing member decides (a weakest-link game). |
| Climate | Battle line / stag hunt or chicken | Taking | Cutting emissions with a smooth cost is a battle line. A known tipping point turns it into a stag hunt or chicken (Taylor's lake that dies past a pollution threshold is chicken; Barrett's argument for a stag hunt is not yet checked). |
| Balinese subak and water temples | Coordination / battle line | Both | Synchronised planting is pure coordination. Sharing water between upstream and downstream is a battle line (Lansing; not yet checked). |
| Frontier AI race | Battle line / stag hunt / chicken | Both | All three, depending on whether labs think racing pays (battle line), would slow if others did (stag hunt), or are waiting for someone else to pause (chicken). |

## What the doubles have in common

Almost all are one of two kinds:

- **Many-player threshold cases.** Below the threshold it is a stag hunt: nobody wants to be the only one. Above it each would rather someone else made up the numbers, which is chicken (Taylor, *The Possibility of Cooperation*, 1987, pp. 41–43). On the map they sit between those two cells, crossing both lines.
- **Cases whose payoffs change with a parameter**: open or limited access, an unseen or a salient disaster, one big player or none. Heckathorn ("The Dynamics and Dilemmas of Collective Action", 1996, p. 257) and Ostrom (*Governing the Commons*, 1990, pp. 46–48) both note that small changes in parameters change the game.

The pattern is in the payoffs, and payoffs move. That is also how fixes work: a fine removes greed, a refund guarantee removes fear, and each moves a case towards "no problem".

## Cases that don't fit

- **Not a dilemma:** meerkat sentinels, probably leaded petrol (both "no problem").
- **Not a payoff structure:** *Twelve Angry Men* (an information cascade); the Aral Sea (a central planning decision); the Ik and the Donner Party (social breakdown); *Jaws* (agency: the mayor).
- **Two parties, not a group:** vampire bats, cleaner fish, acacia ants. These are bilateral exchange; reciprocity and reputation are the remedies.
- **Pattern fits, taking or building doesn't:** the liberum veto, and the two-party cases above.
