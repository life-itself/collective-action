---
title: Curtin et al on Zapotec cooperative institutions
description: Two cooperative institutions in one Oaxacan village run on different norms and different mixes of mechanisms, which supports a cultural evolutionary account of cooperation.
---

Cameron M. Curtin, Narcedalia Vásquez Martinez, Yunitza Vásquez Vásquez and [[Joseph Henrich]], "Zapotec cooperative institutions: Exploring the psychological and social mechanisms". Forthcoming in *Current Anthropology*; version dated 24 Sept 2026. [PDF](https://henrich.fas.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum5811/files/2026-09/Curtin%20et%20al_Social%20and%20psychological%20mechanisms.pdf)

Ethnography, vignettes and surveys on two institutions in San Francisco Yateé, a Zapotec village in Oaxaca: *gozona* (mutual help in farm labor and celebrations) and *usos y costumbres* (traditional governance, including rotating unpaid civic roles that provide public goods).

## Excerpt

> These results support the view that institutions tap into and recombine diverse cooperation-sustaining mechanisms as they culturally evolve, challenging "culture-free" efforts to explain human cooperation. Indeed, even within a single, small community, different cooperative institutions work in different ways.

— Curtin, Vásquez Martinez, Vásquez Vásquez & Henrich, abstract

## Key points

- The claim: norms and institutions play a central role in shaping cooperation, alongside evolved psychology. The authors set this against "culture-free" accounts, which deemphasize culture.
- Each institution has its own norms and drives cooperation in its own context. The norms are not associated with generalized prosociality.
- Both use reputation. Direct reciprocity matters more in gozona, punishment more in usos y costumbres.
- Evidence that the institutions build interdependence is clear only for gozona. Evidence on norm internalization is mixed: ethnography says yes, the priming experiment came out null.
- Market integration, wealth and wage labor are eroding gozona.

## Connections

- Cooperation here is built and maintained by culturally evolved institutions, not something that happens 'naturally'. This is the contrast with accounts that rely on selection alone, such as [[Multi-level selection]]. See [[Cultural group selection]] and [[Gene-culture coevolution]] for the cultural side.
- The mechanisms named ([[Shadow of the future]] for reciprocity, [[Punishment]], reputation) are the ones in [[Cooperation at scale]]. Here they are combined by institutions, differently in each.
- Community institutions that provide public goods: compare [[Ostrom design principles]] in [[Governing the commons]].
- Sits with [[Ultrasociety]], [[Kin networks]] and [[WEIRD]] in [[Cultural evolution]].
